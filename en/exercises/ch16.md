## Oakridge Integrated Project

<a id="ex-ch16-01"></a>

### Exercise 01 — Oakridge Integrated Project

Prepare the diagnosis requested in the management brief. Deliver a notebook with 3–5 figures, five executive conclusions, and 2–4 recommendations, together with decisions, evidence, and limitations. Use the materials below as an investigation and self-assessment guide.

<a id="project-roadmap"></a>

### Project roadmap

This roadmap organizes the investigation without choosing your tools. Record your decisions and preserve a sequence that another person can reproduce. Submit `final_project_firstname_lastname.ipynb`, preferably as one notebook, keeping relative paths to `data/ch16`. In a class, the instructor sets the deadline. For independent study, attempt the project before consulting the solution. Run **Restart Kernel and Run All** before finishing.

<a id="understanding-the-data"></a>

#### 1. Understanding the data

**Guidance.** Understand each table before analyzing results.

**Hint.** What does a row represent? What period is covered? Which variables identify records? Which tables complement one another? Which units of observation differ?

**Caution.** Two tables having a column with the same name does not automatically justify joining them in that way.

<a id="quality"></a>

#### 2. Quality

**Guidance.** Investigate problems that could change the results.

**Hint.** Are there repeated records, missing values, semantically identical categories with different spellings, violations of documented rules, or unusual observations?

**Caution.** Do not change the data before understanding the problem. An extreme observation is not necessarily an error.

<a id="cleaning"></a>

#### 3. Cleaning

**Guidance.** Document each important decision: problem, decision, justification, and possible information loss or change.

**Hint.** Is the decision necessary for every measure or only part of the analysis? Is there a defensible alternative?

**Caution.** There is no universal rule for treating missing values.

<a id="integration"></a>

#### 4. Integration

**Guidance.** Construct coherent analytical data and validate every join.

**Hint.** What is the correct key? What is the cardinality? Should the number of rows change? Do costs vary only by product, or also over time? Do targets belong to transactions or branch × month?

**Caution.** A merge can run without errors and still produce substantively incorrect data.

<a id="indicators"></a>

#### 5. Indicators

**Guidance.** Construct measures that answer the business questions.

**Hint.** Consider activity, revenue, costs, margin, volume, average order value, targets, and change over time. Identify the numerator, denominator, and unit of every ratio.

**Caution.** A larger absolute value does not necessarily mean better performance.

<a id="branches-and-regions"></a>

#### 6. Branches and regions

**Guidance.** Compare units and state the question each measure answers.

**Hint.** Do regions contain the same number of branches? Were all branches active for the same period? Do cumulative totals and average performance answer the same question?

**Caution.** Total comparisons may reflect scale and time exposure.

<a id="products-and-categories"></a>

#### 7. Products and categories

**Guidance.** Investigate the portfolio along more than one dimension.

**Hint.** Does the quantity leader also lead in revenue? Does higher revenue necessarily imply a higher margin? Does sales composition matter?

**Caution.** The “best product” depends on the indicator.

<a id="time"></a>

#### 8. Time

**Guidance.** Analyze activity over the observed period.

**Hint.** Are some months more active? Does activity change between 2024 and 2025? Is a recurring pattern visible?

**Caution.** Describing a trend does not predict the future.

<a id="targets"></a>

#### 9. Targets

**Guidance.** Assess performance against targets and state the denominator.

**Hint.** Meeting a target in one month differs from doing so consistently. Attainment frequency and the actual-to-target ratio answer different questions. How should pre-opening periods be treated?

**Caution.** Structural missingness is not necessarily an error.

<a id="observations-that-warrant-investigation"></a>

#### 10. Observations that warrant investigation

**Guidance.** Identify unusual observations before deciding to retain or remove them.

**Hint.** Do they violate rules? Are their keys valid? Could they represent legitimate business events? What is their impact?

**Caution.** Extreme does not mean erroneous.

<a id="visualizations"></a>

#### 11. Visualizations

**Guidance.** Produce three to five nonredundant visualizations.

**Hint.** For each chart, record the question, visual choice, evidence, interpretation, and limitation. Choose the chart based on the question.

**Caution.** A technically correct chart can be inappropriate for the question or encourage an exaggerated interpretation.

<a id="conclusions-recommendations-and-limitations"></a>

#### 12. Conclusions, recommendations, and limitations

**Guidance.** Present five executive conclusions and two to four recommendations. Each conclusion should follow evidence → interpretation → managerial implication.

**Hint.** Separate what the data show from the action proposed. Include a limitations section addressing synthetic data, descriptive scope, cleaning decisions, coverage, absence of causal identification, and absence of forecasting.

**Caution.** Descriptive association does not demonstrate causality, and an unsupported recommendation is not an analytical conclusion.

<a id="pre-submission-checklist"></a>

### Pre-submission checklist

- [ ] Units of observation have been identified.
- [ ] Cleaning decisions have been justified.
- [ ] Cardinalities have been checked.
- [ ] Numerators, denominators, and units have been stated.
- [ ] Coverage differences have been considered.

<a id="data-dictionary"></a>

### Data dictionary

Oakridge Retail Group is a fictional multiregional retailer and distributor with five U.S. business regions and different product lines. These author-created data are synthetic and educational. The period is January 2024 through December 2025; some branches may have opened during the window. Monetary values are in U.S. dollars, with no implicit thousands scaling. Regions are fictional company groupings, not official Census regions. The U.S. localization preserves the teaching magnitudes without exchange-rate conversion.

<a id="sales.csv"></a>

### sales.csv

Each row represents an order/transaction recorded by a branch.

| Field              | Meaning                                  |
|--------------------|------------------------------------------|
| `order_id`         | Order identifier                         |
| `date`             | Transaction date, YYYY-MM-DD             |
| `branch_id`        | Branch code                              |
| `product_id`       | Product code                             |
| `customer_segment` | Customer segment recorded in the sale    |
| `quantity`         | Units sold                               |
| `unit_price`       | Gross price per unit, in U.S. dollars    |
| `discount_rate`    | Discount as a proportion between 0 and 1 |

Valid discounts range from 0% to 40%, inclusive. There are no returns or negative quantities. Reference segments: Retail, Small Business, and Corporate.

### costs.csv

### targets.csv

Each row represents branch × month. Missing values before opening represent legitimate nonoperation and should not be automatically imputed.

| Field                | Meaning                                       |

| `revenue_target`     | Monthly revenue target, in U.S. dollars       |

<a id="relationships-and-possible-indicators"></a>

### Relationships and possible indicators

- Sales and branches: `branch_id`.
- Sales and products: `product_id`.
- Sales and costs: `product_id` and sale month.
- Monthly actuals and targets: `branch_id` and month.

Before joining, confirm the unit of observation and uniqueness of registry keys. No inference, forecasting, or outside information is needed. The tables support gross revenue, net revenue, total cost, margin, average order value, quantity, and actual-versus-target measures. These derived metrics are not supplied ready-made. Discount and margin-target rates are fractions: 0.10 represents 10%, not 0.10%.

<a id="assessment-rubric"></a>

### Assessment rubric

The rubric assesses decisions, coherence, reproducibility, and communication. Visual appearance alone does not replace conceptual quality. Total: **100%**. The weights are retained; the four levels describe quality without imposing an additional numerical score per level.

<a id="understanding-and-observational-unit-10"></a>

#### 1. Understanding and observational unit — 10%

**Excellent.** Correctly identifies units, period, keys, types, and relationships for every table.

**Adequate.** Identifies the central elements, with minor omissions that do not affect results.

**Partial.** Confuses or omits some units, keys, or coverage.

**Insufficient.** Analyzes without understanding the data structure.

<a id="quality-and-cleaning-15"></a>

#### 2. Quality and cleaning — 15%

**Excellent.** Diagnoses relevant problems and documents decisions, impacts, and alternatives.

**Adequate.** Treats the main problems and justifies most decisions.

**Partial.** Incomplete diagnosis or weakly justified decisions.

**Insufficient.** Ignores quality or alters data arbitrarily.

<a id="transformation-10"></a>

#### 3. Transformation — 10%

**Excellent.** Creates coherent variables and analytical levels while preserving meaning and units.

**Adequate.** Appropriate transformations with minor documentation gaps.

**Partial.** Partly correct or poorly traceable transformations.

**Insufficient.** Incorrect, opaque, or question-incompatible variables.

<a id="integration-and-cardinality-15"></a>

#### 4. Integration and cardinality — 15%

**Excellent.** Identifies units, selects correct keys, understands cardinalities, validates rows, and avoids improper multiplication.

**Adequate.** Joins correctly and performs essential checks.

**Partial.** The merge runs, but cardinality reasoning or validation is weak.

**Insufficient.** Incorrect keys, unjustified many-to-many joins, or invalid analytical data.

<a id="indicators-10"></a>

#### 5. Indicators — 10%

**Excellent.** Defines coherent measures and states numerator, denominator, unit, and analytical level.

**Adequate.** Correct indicators with sufficient documentation.

**Partial.** Some correct indicators, but ambiguities or weak denominators remain.

**Insufficient.** Incorrect indicators or use without understanding.

<a id="exploratory-analysis-10"></a>

#### 6. Exploratory analysis — 10%

**Excellent.** Investigates distributions, groups, time, and unusual observations with clear questions.

**Adequate.** Covers the main patterns and interprets them appropriately.

**Partial.** Limited or mechanical exploration, weakly connected to questions.

**Insufficient.** Merely displays tables or numbers without investigation.

<a id="visualization-10"></a>

#### 7. Visualization — 10%

**Excellent.** Produces 3–5 relevant, legible, interpreted charts with question, evidence, and limitation.

**Adequate.** Appropriate charts that are mostly well communicated.

**Partial.** Redundancy, poor chart choices, or incomplete interpretation.

**Insufficient.** Misleading charts, missing units, or disconnection from the analysis.

<a id="storytelling-8"></a>

#### 8. Storytelling — 8%

**Excellent.** Five conclusions connect evidence, interpretation, and implication without unjustified causal claims.

**Adequate.** Clear, supported conclusions with minor gaps.

**Partial.** Generic, repetitive, or partly unsupported conclusions.

**Insufficient.** Unsupported statements or unjustified causal claims.

<a id="recommendations-7"></a>

#### 9. Recommendations — 7%

**Excellent.** Presents 2–4 plausible actions, separated from evidence and consistent with limitations.

**Adequate.** Coherent recommendations connected to the analysis.

**Partial.** Vague recommendations or weak links to evidence.

**Insufficient.** Recommendations without analytical support or presented as causal findings.

<a id="limitations-and-reproducibility-5"></a>

#### 10. Limitations and reproducibility — 5%

**Excellent.** Records limitations, decisions, and relative paths; Restart + Run All completes without errors.

**Adequate.** Reproducible notebook with the main limitations recorded.

**Partial.** Reproduction requires small fixes, or limitations are incomplete.

**Insufficient.** Nonreproducible notebook or missing limitations.

<a id="serious-conceptual-errors"></a>

### Serious conceptual errors

The following can substantially reduce the assessment of the corresponding dimension:

- An unjustified many-to-many merge.
- Removing an observation only because it is extreme, without investigation and substantive justification.
- Treating structural missingness as an error.
- Imputing discounts without justification.
- Ignoring duplicates.
- Using an indicator without understanding its denominator.
- Confusing total revenue with average performance.
- Ignoring differences in active months.
- Making a causal claim.
- Presenting an unsupported recommendation.
- Submitting a notebook that does not run completely.

<a id="alternative-solutions"></a>

### Alternative solutions

A decision different from the reference pipeline can receive full credit when it is substantively justified, documented, consistently applied, and accompanied by an explanation of its effects on the results.
